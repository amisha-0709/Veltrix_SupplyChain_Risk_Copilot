PS-06 · Supply Chain Resilience Co-Pilot · round 3: Claude interprets, the engine answers · live site, 11 Nov 2026 · synthetic data

Claude now reads the question; the engine writes the answer. All 15 new persona cases pass.

The chat was switched so that Claude's only job is to understand the question (typos, Hindi, Hinglish, comparisons, free-form incidents) and turn it into a structured query for the impact engine. The answer shown is the engine's own fixed-template text, word for word, so it is consistent and every number is reproducible. Claude writes text only when no engine query fits: an order request, an off-topic question, "what does this tool do?". Then 15 new personas, different from rounds 1 and 2, tested it end to end.

Still scripted, not real users. Each case has a written pass rule checked automatically (engine used, Claude's reading, ₹ figures traceable to the engine, no "ordered/approved" claims, case-specific content). Feedback from at least three real people is still needed, using the persona feedback kit.

5 · Limits that remain

  • Claude can misread a question. The reading is shown above every answer so a person can spot it; nothing checks it automatically.
  • Answers come back in English even when the question is in Hindi or Hinglish.
  • Engine answers are terse templates. Consistent and auditable, but less conversational than before.
  • Comparisons don't state a verdict. "China or Taiwan?" shows each side's parts, spend and cover gaps, but the reader decides which is bigger.
  • Questions outside the eight engine queries (e.g. supplier financial health, freight rates) get a short "can't answer that" from Claude rather than an answer.
  • Everything is synthetic. Real news and real users will find new gaps; the pilot's weekly review is where those get caught.
