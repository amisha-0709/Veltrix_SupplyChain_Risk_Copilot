We lost ₹170.26 cr of revenue to disruptions we mostly saw coming. A ₹43.8 lakh pilot tests whether we can stop that.

The problem

In 24 months, 18 disruptions cost ₹170.26 cr of revenue. Most had public warnings one to three weeks ahead, but nobody owns monitoring: the team noticed a median of a day after impact and decided days later. Meanwhile about ₹4.57 cr went on expediting parts whose stock would have lasted anyway. 26 parts have a single supplier, and on most of them stock cover is shorter than the lead time.

What the co-pilot does

Reads news, weather, port and trade notices; links each to our supplier → part → product network; quantifies revenue, margin and OTIF at risk using today's stock and in-transit; ranks options (pre-buy, air freight, switch supplier, re-allocate) by net value with the approver named; and writes a one-page briefing. It recommends; people decide. It never orders, books freight or contacts a supplier.

Evidence

  • Back-test on Feb–Sep 2026, data the rules were not tuned on: every warned disruption caught, a median 14 days ahead, with about 9 alerts a year in total, not a flood.
  • Impact engine reproduces past losses with a 2.8% median error (7 of 9 loss events within ±25%; every no-loss event right).
  • Replay of the Penang MOSFET fire (D05, ₹61 cr lost): same-day briefing and re-allocation would have protected ₹3.68 cr of margin. Qualifying the known alternate beforehand (about ₹15 lakh) would have protected about ₹50 cr of revenue.
  • All 10 acceptance scenarios from the brief pass end to end.

Pilot: 3 months, live feeds, real stock

  • Scope: all suppliers; inbox owned by a named risk analyst (0.5 FTE); weekly 30-minute review with category managers.
  • Cost: ₹35.0 lakh one-off (ERP stock and in-transit feed, set-up) + about ₹8.8 lakh running for the 3 months.
  • Success KPIs: ≥ 80% of relevant signals caught; ≥ 60% of alerts worth acting on; median warning ≥ 7 days; signal to quantified impact in under 5 minutes; impact within ±25% on any real event; a logged decision on every inbox alert within its response time; briefing rated useful (≥ 4/5) by category managers; no expediting where cover was enough.
  • Stop rule: if alert precision is under 60% after week 6, or nobody acts on the briefings, we stop.

Risks

  • Results so far are on synthetic data; real feeds are messier. The pilot exists to measure that.
  • Needs an ERP stock feed; without current cover the impact numbers are guesses.
  • Benefit is concentrated in a few large events; the case holds without them, but thinner.
