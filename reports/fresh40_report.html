PS-06 · Supply Chain Resilience Co-Pilot · fresh test round: 20 "Try a signal" + 20 "Ask the co-pilot" cases · live site, 12 Nov 2026 · synthetic data

The engine is right when it understands the input. It misses wording, spellings and languages it wasn't built on.

Forty cases written fresh for this round (none used before), with the expected behaviour written before running. All ran on the deployed site. Each answer was read and graded by hand. Nothing was changed in the app for or during this round.

1 · Summary

  • Try a signal (14/20): when the word list recognises the wording, linking, quantification and priority are right (S01, S02, S07, S10, S13, S17, S20), and all six "should ignore" stories that look alarming are ignored (denial, a forecast that misses, a threat, good news, an off-network port, market news). It misses everyday disruption wording the synthetic data never used ("walk out", "blocked … runs aground", "load shedding"), misspelt place names and non-English text, and raised one false alarm (gallium/germanium export licences, inbox, ₹46 cr) because the product filter only checks trade notices.
  • Claude's reading of the same signals was right 17/20. It caught the three wording misses (S03, S05, S11). But it is only displayed: the decision still comes from the word list, so the page shows "Claude: relevant" next to "Ignored". Typos and Hindi defeat both, because Claude's extracted names go through the same English-only place list.
  • Ask (12 pass, 5 partial, 3 fail): Claude interpreted every question sensibly, including Hinglish and a supplier named in full. Every partial and fail is a missing engine query, not a misreading: no lookup by part number, no filter by days of cover, no approval-matrix lookup, no joint multi-port scenario, no customer-specific what-if, and answers that leave the key number in the table rather than the sentence.

5 · Read with care

  • Twenty cases per feature is a small sample; the cases were written to probe likely weak spots, so these rates are lower than the scripted rounds and probably lower than typical use.
  • Grading is by one reader against expectations written beforehand; borderline calls (watch log vs inbox) were counted as alerts.
  • Everything runs on synthetic data and today's stock (30 Sep 2026).
