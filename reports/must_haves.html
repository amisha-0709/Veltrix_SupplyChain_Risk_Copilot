1 · Signal triage

Brief: Signal triage: classify every signal as relevant or not and extract the locations, companies, ports and commodities it mentions.

How it's met: Every one of the 3,609 signals (3,017 news, 438 weather alerts, 154 trade notices) is scored by a rule lexicon and linked to the network by a gazetteer of ports, countries, regions, chokepoints, locations, suppliers and commodities. Claude reviewed the borderline band (8 stories), lifting test recall from 95.3% to 100% with no new false alarms. In Try a signal, Claude also reads each new signal live and extracts its status, event type, locations, ports, routes, companies and commodities, which the gazetteer then links (that live extraction is not part of the back-test below).

Evidence: Test-period signals: recall 100.0%, precision 100% (43 of 43 relevant, 0 false). Extraction on the 179 relevant, disruption-linked signals: the extracted entities point at a part the disruption actually hit for 95% (88% in the test months); the disruption's place is extracted for 94%; commodities are right 100% of the time (59 signals name one). met

2 · Entity linking

Brief: Entity linking: map each relevant signal to affected suppliers, parts and SKUs through the bill of materials.

How it's met: Linked nodes are traced supplier → part → SKU → customer through the BOM graph. Place ∩ commodity is intersected (Penang ∩ Semiconductors). Signals that name nothing of ours are never guessed onto the network: they join an open event or go to Needs review.

Evidence: Alerts cover every part the disruption actually hit for 17 of 18 disruptions; lowest 74% (D09: 12 hit parts ship via Chennai per the shipments table, so lane-based linking can't reach them). Median SKU recall 100%. Linked sets are deliberately broader than what was hit (exposure first; the impact engine then finds which parts actually run short). met

3 · Impact quantification following the playbook

Brief: Impact quantification following the playbook: effective delay vs days of cover, shortage days, units short, revenue and margin at risk, customer OTIF.

How it's met: core/impact.py implements playbook §3 step by step; every alert carries the working.

Evidence: Replaying D01–D18 with recorded durations and cover at the time: median error 2.8%, 7/9 loss events within ±25%, 9/9 no-loss events correctly predicted no loss. met

4 · Mitigation ranking

Brief: Mitigation ranking: options with cost, time to effect, net value and the approver the approval matrix requires.

How it's met: core/mitigate.py: all playbook options per part, net value = margin avoided × P(works in time) − cost − penalties anyway, on-time and shortage-fixing options first, approver from the matrix by decision type and cost.

Evidence: 37 options across the 19 alerts: 0 missing a field, 0 with an approver that differs from the matrix. met

5 · A back-test on the signal labels

Brief: A back-test on the signal labels: precision, recall and lead time on the test period (from 1 Feb 2026).

How it's met: core/backtest.py replays every signal in date order; rules tuned on train only.

Evidence: Test period: signal recall 100.0% (target 80%), alert precision 100% (target 60%), median lead 13.75 days (target 7), all 4 of 4 warned disruptions caught. met

6 · A one-page briefing per alert that a Head of Supply Chain could forward without editing.

Brief: A one-page briefing per alert that a Head of Supply Chain could forward without editing.

How it's met: core/briefing.py: headline impact, the decision asked with approvers, what is happening (strongest signal), exposure, products, customers, recommended options, what's left after the plan, sources, assumptions; HTML for print and plain text for email.

Evidence: 19 of 19 briefings pass all 10 automated checks (longest 2,328 characters). Whether a real Head of Supply Chain would forward them unedited still needs the persona sessions. met

7 · A working interface deployed at a public URL.

Brief: A working interface deployed at a public URL.

How it's met: https://veltrix-risk-copilot.vercel.app: FastAPI on Vercel, static UI, no sign-in.

Evidence: 11 of 11 pages and API endpoints returned 200 without signing in when this report was built. met

12 · Limits

  • All data is synthetic and templated; the rule lexicon was built against it, so real feeds will score lower. The persona sessions and a pilot are the real test.
  • Claude's extraction of every signal (rules vs Claude vs both, chosen on train only) is built but has not been run on the full history (about $3.40 of API calls), so must-have 1's figures above are for the rules plus Claude's review of borderline signals.
  • D09's 74% part recall reflects a mismatch in the data pack: parts recorded as hit by the Nhava Sheva flooding ship via Chennai.
